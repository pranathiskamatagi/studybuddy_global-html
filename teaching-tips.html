How to explain effectively

A few quick techniques to help your session go smoothly.

1

Start with a curious question 🤔

Open with a question that sparks curiosity instead of diving straight into the explanation.

e.g. "Why do you think ice floats instead of sinking?"

2

Let them think first 🧠

Give them a moment to guess or reason it out before you explain - it sticks better that way.

e.g. Pause a few seconds after asking, even if they seem unsure.

3

Then explain the topic 📖

Once they've had a chance to think, walk them through the real explanation clearly.

e.g. "Okay, here's what's actually going on..."

4

Break it into small parts 🧩

Divide the whole topic into small pieces and explain them one at a time, not all at once.

e.g. Teaching photosynthesis? Cover sunlight, then water and CO₂, then glucose, then oxygen - one step at a time.

5

Use as many examples as possible ✏️

The more examples you give, the more likely one of them will click for your partner.

e.g. For a formula, work it out with 2-3 different numbers, not just one.

6

Bring in real-life examples 🌍

Connect the topic to something from everyday life so it feels less abstract.

e.g. Explaining percentages? Use a real discount, like "20% off a ₹500 shirt."

7

Ask them for a real-life example 💭

Turn it around - ask your partner to come up with their own real-life example of the concept.

e.g. "Can you think of somewhere you've seen this used in real life?"

8

Ask each other questions 🔁

Keep it a two-way conversation - both of you should be asking questions, not just one person explaining.

e.g. "Does that make sense so far?" - and let them ask you anything that's still unclear.

9

Try solving a problem together 🎯

If it fits the topic, pick a real problem and work through it together using what you just covered.

e.g. Just explained the Pythagorean theorem? Find the missing side of an actual triangle together.
